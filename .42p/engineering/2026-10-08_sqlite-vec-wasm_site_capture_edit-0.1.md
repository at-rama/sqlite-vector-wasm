# Site Capture

## Purpose, scope, and authority

Retain the reconciled design of A-Site, the public web surface of sqlite-vector-wasm: presentation, usage, browser demonstration and GitHub/npm access. This Capture projects instituted user decisions from S-site-design, S-site-exploration and S-site-mission, with the bounded navigation addition from S-site-npm, the Usage-page addition from S-site-usage, scoped identity/composition consequences from S-product-design and the publication references reconciled under S-capture-reconciliation; S-institution now adopts its reconciled content as the current design reference for this scope, without giving it power to institute its own revisions or prove implementation. It is distinct from the [Distribution Capture](2026-10-04_sqlite-vec-wasm_capture_edit-0.1.md) and [Release/Watch Capture](2026-10-09_sqlite-vec-wasm_release-watch_capture_edit-0.1.md), which retain product obligations and the expressly revised versioning/watch design. These related Captures share provenance and do not independently corroborate history or implementation.

S-site-mission replaces S-site-design's proposed integration into a living root specification with two Captures and one global Allocation, and originally instituted one `A-site` unit. S-site-split replaces that allocation choice with `A-site_shell`, `A-site_docs` and `A-site_demo`; A-site remains a descriptive grouping without its own realization Change. Realization is deferred to the respective OpenSpec Changes. This design migration implements neither the site nor its build or deployment. Source design requirements below retain their force; permissions, reported observations, unselected alternatives and unresolved choices are identified separately. S-engine-neutral replaces component-specific SQL names by the same selected-engine execution, identity and independent-check obligations; it changes no corpus, representation, metric, interaction, persistence boundary or publication-consumption rule.

S-product-engine selects SQLite-Vector for future product documentation and demo adaptation, superseding the earlier two-composition orientation. Existing sqlite-vec code and historical published-runtime evidence are not a second current offering. The transition does not relabel old releases or permit a demo built from unpublished sources.

S-composition-cleanup supersedes S-retained-capability only for abandoned-engine technical retention: the single active composition is SQLite plus SQLite-Vector. Remove sqlite-vec acquisition, build, packaging, qualification and obsolete compatibility paths. Historical releases, source revisions, archives and evidence retain their exact identities; historical reconstruction uses the recorded historical revision and prerequisites. Current tooling retains only narrowly necessary read-only interpretation of actual historical publication records for transition, channel/reference, integrity and uniqueness rules; old records cannot become current candidates or be relabeled as Vector. S-release-review revises candidate documentation preparation and deployment as detailed below. These primary human decisions govern only their stated scope; this reconciliation records them without instituting other model choices.

S-demo-placeholder authorizes a temporary replacement of the running demo by a static refactoring notice and a link to Usage. This scoped exception supersedes continued exposure of the old pinned demo and suspends the runnable-demo duties below until its separately qualified return; it neither removes those future duties nor adopts the broader composition-cleanup amendments.

### C-emoji — Corpus identity and limits

Source: S-site-design, “Corpus”.

Use the complete scientific [uclnlp/emoji2vec corpus](https://github.com/uclnlp/emoji2vec): 1,661 emojis with precomputed 300-dimensional embeddings. Its enriched descriptions supply English names and associated words across the corpus. Render Unicode characters through the visitor's system; no image collection is needed. Freeze the data to an identified upstream revision and retain its MIT notice and source credit. The corpus is old and lacks recent emojis; French labels are outside the initial scope. The specific upstream revision and prepared-data file formats are not selected here.

### C-vector-modes — Default binary and optional Float32

Source: S-site-design, “Deux modes vectoriels”; S-site-exploration.

Binary is the default: one bit per component according to its sign, padded from 300 to 304 dimensions, using Hamming distance and loaded at startup. The four padding dimensions are identical across vectors and do not contribute to distances. Float32 uses the original 300-dimensional vectors and cosine distance, loaded on first mode selection and retained in memory thereafter. Both modes use the same emojis and identifiers. Switching mode preserves the selected emoji and reruns its search. Quantization can materially change the neighbors; semantic relevance is not an acceptance criterion.

### C-interaction — Small exploratory interface

Source: S-site-design, “Interface de démonstration”.

Provide a text-search field for names and associated words, a grid of matching emojis, selection of an emoji to show its six vector neighbors excluding itself, clickable neighbors for continued exploration, an emoji-copy button and a **Binaire / Float32** selector. Distances and technical control information are accessible in a collapsible panel. This defines behavior without choosing an interface library or internal component structure.

### C-search — Text starting point and actual vector execution

Source: S-site-design, “Interface de démonstration” and “Stockage et durée de vie”.

Text search finds a starting emoji; vector search uses its existing embedding. Data are imported into SQLite, and vector searches in the future product demo are executed by SQLite-Vector through its native SQL; the existing published-runtime transition boundary in C-demo-release still applies. No free-form sentence is vectorized, and no model, remote embedding service or API key is necessary. The internal textual-search mechanism is not selected.

### C-memory — Transient connection lifetime

Source: S-site-design, “Stockage et durée de vie”; S-site-exploration.

The database is exclusively in memory; explicit connection closure destroys it. No authentication, local persistence or OPFS file cleanup is needed. This reconciles the early “Flush à la déconnexion” intention with the designated in-memory design without inventing a login/logout flow or persistence test.

### C-demo-checks — Scoped deterministic verification

Source: S-site-design, “Vérification et limites”; S-site-usage, Usage rendering/links/example checks; S-site-npm, package-link and mobile-menu checks.

Compare a few searches in each mode deterministically with expected results calculated independently of the selected vector engine. On the exercised paths, verify loading of the published WASM, automatic extension availability, vector insertion and searches. Surprising neighborhoods alone do not prove vector execution. Also verify navigation, README rendering and links from GitHub Pages. Check the npm link’s package destination and its adjacency to GitHub in the common navigation, including access through the mobile menu. Verify Usage rendering and links, and that its minimal example works with the distribution version it announces: exact qualified unpublished candidate bytes for the identified release preview, matching actually published bytes for stable public deployment. Candidate check results remain revision/archive-bound and do not replace post-integration qualification.

During the placeholder phase, verify the notice and Usage link, common navigation/mobile access, and absence of demo runtime/Worker/corpus loading or preparation dependencies on local and identified Pages builds. Runtime/vector checks are deferred, not passed or replaced by placeholder checks; shell/docs and distribution acceptance retain their applicable obligations.

The demo complements product acceptance; it establishes neither OPFS, persistence nor the complete distributed API/capability surface. Distribution acceptance remains allocated separately, with no weakened gate. This PR supplies no browser-demo result or independent oracle execution.

## Rationale, alternatives and open choices

### R-demo — Small web-friendly demonstration

Source: S-site-exploration; S-site-design, “Taille et faisabilité”.

Emoji name search addresses a familiar web problem; vector neighbors make an easy exploratory interaction. The user prioritizes demonstrating that the selected vector engine and the published WASM function, rather than optimizing semantic relevance. Binary-first loading keeps the initial data small; optional Float32 lets visitors observe representation differences. Imperfect neighbors are acceptable, but C-demo-checks supplies the actual verification basis.

Reported exploratory sizes, excluding JS/WASM and UI, are approximately 133 kB for compact emoji/name/word JSON, 63 kB for binary vectors, 196 kB combined initially or 94 kB with gzip, and 2 MB for optional Float32 vectors. These are the source's reported upstream-file/reference-calculation observations, not renewed measurements, transfer guarantees or browser/SQLite results. JSON here describes the measured representation, not a prescribed demo-data format. The source reports no verification of the future browser page.

### O-corpus — Earlier miniature corpus alternative

Source: S-site-exploration; superseding selection in S-site-design, “Corpus”.

An approximately hundred-item miniature dataset was initially sought; the full emoji2vec corpus is now selected because the binary representation remains small and offers the desired emoji exploration. A miniature subset is unselected, not technically refuted. The user rejected a commercially branded unrelated source to avoid promotion; the academic source is the adopted orientation. No alternative dataset becomes a second demo obligation.

### Q-volume — Final SQLite size

Source: S-site-design, “Taille et faisabilité”.

The final database volume with the selected engine's vector storage remains to be measured. Existing file-size/reference experiments do not settle it. No size threshold or readiness blocker has been instituted.

### Q-realization — OpenSpec realization choices

Source: S-site-mission, Capture Site and Allocation instructions; S-site-split, adopted responsibility boundaries and dependencies.

Framework, repository file organization, build/deployment pipeline, interface library, prepared-data formats, selected upstream revision, internal text/vector-search mechanisms and deterministic fixture details remain realization choices for the respective Changes and Apply, constrained by the behaviors above. The adopted units are `A-site_shell` (static construction/deployment, common navigation/mobile styling), `A-site_docs` (README presentation and Usage), and `A-site_demo` (the complete published-runtime demonstration, including data, both representations, interactions and independent vector checks). Each owns its scoped checks; shell also verifies that documentation-only updates preserve the demo runtime identity. Shell precedes integration of docs and demo, which can then be realized independently; neither requires the other's content or runtime. A-site is only their descriptive grouping, with no residual realization responsibility or Change. No further data/UI/search subdivision or new passage condition is instituted merely because realization choices remain open.
